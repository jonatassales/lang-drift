# Home product sections and the Book a demo page

**Status:** wontfix. The owner closed it on 2026-11-05: superseded by ADR 0005 (Perfect Simulation Alignment).

## Problem Statement

The home page of the platform site is the hero and a grid of four product cards, "Four products. One platform." A visitor learns a product's name and one sentence about it there, and nothing of what it does; the demos that show it live three clicks away on the product pages. The products are listed in an order, Vision, FinOps, Microruntimes, AI Voice, that no longer reflects how the owner presents the platform. The one call to action, "Book a Demo", goes nowhere: the header button is plain text, the hero link jumps to a paused band on the Vision page titled "Ask for a Demo", and the same paused band closes every product page. A visitor who wants a demo has no page to ask for one.

## Solution

The home page shows each product after the hero, in the order Vision, Microruntimes, FinOps, AI Voice, each in the same skeleton: the product's icon and name on a row of their own at the right, a question as the heading and a description under it at the left, the product's own demo, and, at the end, a link to the product page. Vision's section is the Vision page's hero as it is: the "Why did your Product Vision fall…" question, its description, the Product Vision curve beside the Sinapsi graph, the caption and the summary. Microruntimes shows its simulated planning session, the terminal driving the ticket, plan and workflow cards, under a question of its own. FinOps shows the budget ledger with the delivery receipt docked beside it under its envelope statement. AI Voice shows the orb playing the scripted sample answer; it has no page yet, so no link. The card grid goes. A fifth section is a FAQ for the whole platform: tabs for Vision, Microruntimes and FinOps switch one accordion between the questions each product page already answers. The page closes with a "Book a demo" band.

"Book a demo" replaces "Ask for a demo" everywhere, and every demo call to action, the header's rainbow button, the hero's link, the home's closing band and the band that closes the Vision, Microruntimes and FinOps pages, opens a new page, `/book-a-demo/`, in every locale. The page holds a form: a company email address, which must be valid and must not belong to a free-mail provider, the company's name and the visitor's position, chosen from a list of twelve. A submission is validated on the server with the same rules; until the request has a destination the server answers that requests are paused and the form shows the "we'll be back soon" notice, as early access does today. The pricing page keeps its early-access band.

## User Stories

1. As a visitor, I want the home page to show each product with its own demo, so that I understand what each one does without leaving the page.
2. As a visitor, I want the products in the order Vision, Microruntimes, FinOps, AI Voice on the home page, in the Platform menu, in the drawer and in the hero's product column, so that the platform reads in one order everywhere.
3. As a visitor, I want each product section to open with the product's icon and name at the right, so that I know which product I am looking at before I read.
4. As a visitor, I want each product section to ask a question and answer it in one paragraph, so that the demo under it has a point.
5. As a visitor, I want Vision's section to be the question, description and demo I know from the Vision page, so that nothing I learnt there changes.
6. As a visitor, I want to select a marked point on the Product Vision curve or a node in the graph on the home page and see the other follow, so that the demo works as it does on the Vision page.
7. As a visitor, I want Microruntimes' section to play the planning session into its ticket, plan and workflow cards, so that I see the harness at work.
8. As a visitor, I want FinOps' section to show the ledger with the receipt beside it and following the selected work item, so that I see the envelope, the reservation and the receipt as one instrument.
9. As a visitor, I want the unknown spend in FinOps' section to say unknown and never zero, so that a missing charge never looks like no charge.
10. As a visitor, I want AI Voice's section to show the orb and let me play the sample answer, so that I see what asking about a change looks like.
11. As a visitor, I want AI Voice's section to say the preview uses no microphone and generates no live answer, so that I never mistake it for a working product.
12. As a visitor, I want each section that has a product page to end with a link to it, so that I can go deeper when a demo interests me.
13. As a visitor, I want the illustrative captions and the simulated figures to say they are illustrative on the home page as on the product pages, so that I never take a demo for a measurement.
14. As a visitor, I want one FAQ on the home page with a tab per product, so that I find a product's answers without opening its page.
15. As a visitor, I want switching a FAQ tab to show that product's questions collapsed, so that an answer I opened for one product does not stay open under another.
16. As a keyboard user, I want the FAQ tabs to move with the arrow keys and the accordion to open with Enter, so that the section works without a pointer.
17. As a visitor, I want the home page to close with a "Book a demo" band, so that the next step is in front of me when I finish reading.
18. As a visitor, I want the header's "Book a Demo" button to open the Book a demo page, on the bar and in the drawer, so that the one highlighted action does something.
19. As a visitor on a narrow screen, I want the drawer to close when I tap "Book a Demo" in it, so that the page I asked for is not hidden behind it.
20. As a visitor, I want the hero's "Book a Demo" link to open the Book a demo page, so that the home's two buttons both lead somewhere.
21. As a visitor, I want the band that closes the Vision, Microruntimes and FinOps pages to say "Book a demo" and lead to the page, so that I never read "Ask for a demo" again.
22. As a visitor, I want the Vision page's in-page links that offered a demo to reach the band at the bottom of that page, so that no link points at nothing.
23. As a visitor, I want the Book a demo page in my language, so that I read it as I read the rest of the site.
24. As a visitor, I want the form to ask for my company email, my company's name and my position, so that a demo request carries what a demo needs.
25. As a visitor, I want to pick my position from a list, so that I do not have to type it.
26. As a visitor, I want a free-mail address such as a Gmail address refused with a message that says so, so that I know the form wants a company address.
27. As a visitor, I want each field's error next to the field, in my language, when I leave it empty or invalid, so that I can fix it before submitting.
28. As a visitor, I want the submit button disabled while my request is being sent, so that I do not send it twice.
29. As a visitor, I want the form to tell me that demo requests are paused and will be back soon when the server cannot take them, so that I know what happened to my request.
30. As a visitor, I want the form kept with my values and a plain failure message when the request fails for any other reason, so that I can try again.
31. As a screen-reader user, I want each product section labelled by its heading and described by its caption and summary, so that I know what the demo under it shows.
32. As a screen-reader user, I want the form's errors and status announced, so that I learn what happened without hunting for it.
33. As a visitor with reduced motion, I want the chroma headline still and the answer card to appear without sliding, so that the page respects my preference.
34. As a visitor in the light scheme, I want the voice preview's borders and muted text in the scheme's colors, so that the section reads in light as in dark.
35. As a search engine, I want the Book a demo page to carry its own title, description, canonical, hreflang and sitemap entries in every locale, so that it indexes correctly.
36. As the owner, I want the new Microruntimes question, the FinOps description, the section links, the FAQ heading and the Book a demo copy drafted in five locales, so that I edit catalogs rather than start from nothing.
37. As the owner, I want a demo request validated on the server before it is refused, so that a destination can be added without changing the form's contract.
38. As the owner, I want no company email, name or position to be stored, logged or sent anywhere while requests are paused, so that the pause is a pause.
39. As a frontend developer, I want one section shell shared by the four product sections, so that a change to the skeleton happens once.
40. As a frontend developer, I want the FAQ tabs to reuse the three product FAQ catalogs, so that no question is translated twice.
41. As a frontend developer, I want the form's validation rules in one schema used by the form and the route, so that the client and the server agree.
42. As a frontend developer, I want the field errors to be codes mapped to the catalog, so that the schema holds no copy.
43. As a frontend developer, I want the card grid, its vendored block and the voice preview's stylesheet removed rather than parked, so that the dead-code check and the shrink-only stylesheet rule stay honest.
44. As a frontend developer, I want every new string in the JSON catalogs with parity across the five locales, so that the localization checks stay green.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Order.** The platform products' list, which the Platform menu, the drawer and the hero's product column already read, is reordered to Vision, Microruntimes, FinOps, AI Voice. The home sections follow the same list, so the order is written once.
- **The card grid goes.** `PlatformFeatures` is deleted with its catalog keys, and the vendored SmoothUI Features Grid and the Glow Hover Card it alone used leave the web package with their export entries: after this change nothing renders them, and the dead-code check would report them.
- **Section shell.** A new `HomeProductSection` Server Component in a new home components group renders the skeleton: a landmark with a fragment id, labelled by its heading and described by its optional caption and screen-reader summary; the product's icon in the small bordered chip the Microruntimes heading and the Platform menu use, and the product's name from the header catalog, on a row of their own aligned right; a level-two heading in the Vision hero's heading style (the same clamp, weight, tracking and line height, left-aligned, balanced); a lead in the Vision hero's lead style; the demo as children; and an optional link in the site's text-link style with the arrow icon at the end. It takes the product entry from the products list, the heading as a node (so Vision's chroma headline fits), the description, the optional caption and summary, and the optional link. The shell owns no color but role utilities, no stylesheet.
- **Vision section.** Composes the shell with the Vision page's hero copy as it is: the rich question with `ChromaText` (formatted synchronously from the home catalog so the section stays a plain Server Component), its description, caption and summary, and the Product Vision curve beside the Sinapsi graph in a Tailwind twin of the Vision hero's demo grid: the two columns from the `lg` breakpoint, stacked and centered up to 780px below it. The Vision page keeps its own hero and stylesheet; the graph, like the receipt, takes an optional heading level that defaults to its page's, so the home renders it under a section heading. The chroma headline stands still under reduced motion through the vendored stylesheet's own rule. The link goes to the Vision page.
- **Microruntimes section.** Composes the shell with a new question and description from the platform catalog and the Microruntimes page's demo: the session terminal and the session cards with the same props and the same grid as the page. The summary is the page's hero summary. The link goes to the Microruntimes page.
- **FinOps section.** Composes the shell with the FinOps envelope statement as its heading, each sentence on its own line, a new description from the platform catalog, the budget ledger and the docked delivery receipt with the page's props and grid, the page's caption and summary. The link goes to the FinOps page.
- **AI Voice section.** Composes the shell with the voice title and description from the home catalog and `VoicePreview` as the demo; no link, since the product has no page. `VoicePreview` is trimmed to the orb, the question row and the answer card: its kicker, heading and paragraph leave, because the shell renders them. Its styling moves to role utilities (the stylesheet hard-codes dark-only colors that break in the light scheme), the stylesheet is deleted, its line leaves the frontend rule's shrink-only list and the kicker's dark modifier class leaves the global stylesheet; the component leaves the dead-code allow list because the home renders it.
- **FAQ.** A new `PlatformFaqSection` Server Component builds three groups, Vision, Microruntimes and FinOps, from the id lists and FAQ items the three product catalogs already hold (the Vision list moves to a data module the Vision FAQ section reads, so the home takes no stylesheet from it), labels them with the header's product names and renders the product FAQ's shell (heading, lead, fragment id `faq`) around a new `FaqTabs` Client Component: SmoothUI's Animated Tabs, controlled, with a tab panel per group wired by the tabs' ids, as the Microruntimes scenarios do; the Basic Accordion is rendered only inside the active panel, so switching tabs mounts a fresh collapsed accordion. The heading, lead and tab-list label are new platform copy.
- **Book a demo page.** A new indexable route, `book-a-demo`, in every locale: skip link, header, a level-one title and a lead from a new `book-a-demo` catalog, the form, the compact footer. Its metadata entry joins the pages catalog under the same id, which resolves to its path without a mapping, and the sitemap gains its five URLs with the six alternates.
- **Form.** A new `BookDemoForm` Client Component built with react-hook-form and the zod resolver, new dependencies of the website pinned exactly, the newest releases the repository's release-age policy accepts. Fields: company email, company name, position as a native select whose first option is an empty placeholder. Validation runs on touch and on submit through one schema: the email reuses the early-access email rules (one definition of a valid address) and refuses an address whose domain is in a set of free-mail domains; the company is trimmed, required and at most 120 characters; the position must be one of the twelve ids. Every issue's message is an error code; the form maps codes to the catalog's `errors` entries and shows each under its field with `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby`. The submit button reads the form's own submitting state. The form posts JSON with the three fields and the locale to the route. A 503 replaces the form with the return-soon notice and focuses its heading; any other failure keeps the form and its values and announces a failure message in a live region.
- **Positions.** Twelve ids in code, labels in the catalog: founder, CEO, CTO, VP of Engineering, VP of Product, engineering manager, product manager, staff engineer, software engineer, platform engineer, finance, other.
- **Route.** A new `book-a-demo` route handler on the Node runtime: `POST` reads a body bounded as the early-access route bounds it, validates with the schema extended by the locale and closed to other keys, answers 400 with the field codes on failure, and otherwise answers 503 with `Retry-After`, `Cache-Control: no-store` and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, the body `{ ok: false, code: 'UNAVAILABLE' }`. Nothing is stored, logged or forwarded. Validation runs first so that a destination can be added behind the same contract; a comment at the refusal says so.
- **Band.** A new `BookDemoBand` Server Component, fragment id `book-a-demo`, in the early-access band's card: the title "Book a demo", a description (the band's own, or a sentence the page passes), and a pill link to the page. It closes the home after the FAQ and replaces the landing early-access band on the Vision, Microruntimes and FinOps pages; FinOps passes its own sentence. `EarlyAccessSection` keeps only its pricing branch; the landing branch, its description prop and the "Ask for a Demo" keys are removed. The Vision page's three in-page links that pointed at the early-access fragment point at the band's.
- **Entry points.** The header's demo button becomes a link to the page, keeping its rainbow ring, with an optional click handler the drawer uses to close itself; the hero's secondary call to action links to the page.
- **Copy.** The platform catalog loses `features` and gains `sections` (Vision's link; Microruntimes' question, description and link; FinOps' description and link) and `faq` (title, description, tab-list label). The early-access catalog loses the two demo keys. A new `book-a-demo` catalog, five locales in one file, holds the skip link, title, lead, the band's title, description and call to action, the form's labels, placeholders, submit and submitting labels, failure message and return-soon notice, the twelve position labels keyed by id and the error messages keyed by code. The pages catalog gains the page's title and description. "Product Vision" stays in English; "Book a Demo" keeps the header's casing. All drafted copy is marked for the owner's review.
- **Workflow.** One commit per ticket on `feat/home-product-sections` with its `Ticket:` trailer and no AI attribution lines, then a pull request into `main`.

## Testing Decisions

A good test checks what a visitor or a build sees: rendered text, ARIA state, the store value after a click, a response's status and body, a catalog's parity. It never asserts class names or internal structure.

Seams, highest first:

1. **Catalog parity** (existing seam): the `book-a-demo` family joins the website's catalog test, which checks keys, ICU arguments and rich tags against `en` and formats every message with counts 0, 1, 2, 3 and 12 in every locale; the platform and early-access families stay in it with their changed keys. The catalog index's shape assertion covers key order. Two bridges in the schema test tie code to copy: the error codes equal the keys of the English `errors`, the position ids equal the keys of the English `positions`.
2. **Schema** (new test at the early-access validation seam): each failure yields its code (required, invalid, too long, free mail with mixed case and country-code domains, company required and too long, position required and unknown); every position id passes; the submission schema refuses unknown keys and an unknown locale.
3. **Route** (new test like the early-access route tests): malformed JSON answers 400 and an oversized body 413, as the early-access route bounds it; a bad field answers 400 with its code; a valid submission answers 503 with the three headers and the body; no network call leaves the handler.
4. **Form** (new jsdom test like the early-access form test, with the real English catalog): an empty submit shows the three catalog errors and sends nothing; a Gmail address shows the free-mail error; valid data posts one JSON body with the locale; a stubbed 503 swaps the form for the return-soon notice; a stubbed 500 keeps the form and shows the failure message.
5. **Section shell and band** (new jsdom tests like the early-access section test): the shell shows the product name, labels the landmark by its heading, describes it by caption and summary when given, and shows the link only when given; the band shows the title, the given or default description and the link to the page.
6. **FAQ tabs** (new jsdom test like the Microruntimes scenarios test): selecting Microruntimes shows its first question and none of Vision's; an item expanded under one tab is collapsed after switching away and back; arrow keys move the selection.
7. **Voice preview** (existing seam): the jsdom test keeps every state and timer case; only the heading and kicker assertions leave with the elements.
8. **Early-access section** (existing seam): the landing cases leave with the branch; the pricing case stays.
9. **Repository checks**: `pnpm turbo run check`, `pnpm turbo run typecheck test --filter=website --filter=@langdrift/web`, `pnpm turbo run build --filter=website`, `drift check localization`, `drift check dead-code`.

Manual verification in the built-in browser: `/en/` from top to bottom at desktop width and at 375px, in light and dark, with no horizontal overflow: hero, Vision, Microruntimes, FinOps, AI Voice, FAQ, band, footer, each label at the right and each link last; the hero column, the Platform menu and the drawer in the new order; the curve and the graph following each other; the orb playing the sample answer; the FAQ tabs switching and resetting; `/en/book-a-demo/` refusing an empty form and a Gmail address, then showing the return-soon notice; the header button, the hero link and the four bands landing on the page; the drawer closing on tap; `/en/platform/vision/` unchanged above its band with its three in-page links reaching it; `/en/pricing/` keeping its early-access band; `/en/sitemap.xml` listing 35 URLs; `/pt-br/`, `/zh-hant/`, `/ja/` and `/vi/` rendering every new section.

## Out of Scope

- An AI Voice product page, and a destination for demo requests: a registry endpoint, an email provider or a calendar.
- Reopening sign-up in the pricing page's early-access band.
- The footer's FAQ link, which still points at the Vision page's FAQ.
- The Vision, Microruntimes and FinOps pages above their closing band.
- Deferring the home's demos for first paint; the terminal already starts on intersection, and the rest is measured after the change.
